# Outline 
### Summary 
Every day, we discard waste. Cardboard, food, water bottles, packaging, you name it! While some waste decays over weeks or months, the plastics consumed today will outlive us in landfills. According to Our World in Data, 350,000,000 tonnes of plastic waste are created each year. In data visualization, color choice plays a critical role in framing narrative intent; specifically, blue is universally used to represent recycling, clean water, and trust. This project will take advantage of that visual convention, contrasting the clean “recycling blue” narrative against the “big bad warning” data of global waste accumulation (orange).

Behind that recycling narrative lies a massive global export pipeline that shifts the crisis out of sight. High-income nations promote domestic sustainability programs while quietly exporting millions of tons of plastic waste to developing countries across the Global South. By mapping these international trade flows, the project visualizes how regulatory loopholes under international agreements allow wealthy nations to outsource their non-degradable burden, sending ocean freighters packed with synthetic scrap to lower-income recipient nations. 

In these recipient communities, the overwhelming influx of cheap plastic waste creates horrible economic and public health realities. In parts of Southeast Asia and Latin America, discarded plastic has become cheaper than natural firewood, driving local food industries, such as local tofu production facilities, to burn plastic waste as fuel. This section highlights the severe biological cost of this survival tactic, illustrating how combustion releases airborne carcinogens.

The remaining part of the story pivots to show a light at the end of the tunnel. Anchored by the Recycled Orchestra of Cateura (featured in Landfill Harmonic) and the world premiere of the musical Basura in Atlanta, the story highlights how community innovation and artistic storytelling can force global attention (and hopefully one day accountability) onto this catastrophe.
