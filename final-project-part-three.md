## The audience
I identified my primary audience as theater-goers who are attending or have just seen Basura the Musical, as well as eco-conscious people who want to understand their personal environmental impact better. Early feedback and user research helped me realize that talking about all municipal trash in general was way too broad, so I narrowed my focus specifically to plastic waste to give the narrative a sharper, more actionable edge.

Because my audience might be reading this right before entering the theater or right after leaving the show, I kept the entire piece spoiler-free so it works as an easy companion guide. To make sure the connection between the theatrical performance and the data was crystal clear, I added a brand-new concluding sentence that ties everything together: "Just as Basura turns discarded waste into vibrant rhythm and theater, looking at the real path of our plastic forces us to confront how our everyday habits impact communities half a world away."

## Final design decisions
When it came to my final graph choices, I completely re-evaluated my approach from Part 2. Instead of using generic charts about all types of municipal waste, I switched to a map that highlights the top 10 plastic exporting countries. This change made a huge difference because it narrowed my focus to plastic specifically. It was hard for me to get narrower because I want to follow the water bottle to the plate. To make the data connect directly back to Basura, I also highlighted how plastic gets repurposed into musical instruments in waste-receiving cities, like using old X-ray film as the skin for a drumhead. Showing how communities turn discarded plastic into music creates a tangible link between global trade data and the creative spirit of the show.

For the overall color palette, I relied heavily on the color orange. It directly matches the official Basura logo, but I also chose it based on what we learned in class about color theory and emotional response. In American culture, reds and oranges naturally signal caution, urgency, or a mess. I intentionally used these bright red and orange tones across my graphs and callouts to evoke those exact feelings in my readers, pushing them to feel the urgency of the plastic crisis rather than just looking at passive numbers.

Finally, I designed the website layout so that the images tell a complete narrative on their own. I started with a mood board and picked photos in Shorthand that already had built-in annotations, which made them easy to cite while keeping the layout clean and professional. The images follow a step-by-step visual chain: a single water bottle getting tossed in a bin, loaded onto an export ship, sorted through by Indonesian villagers in massive landfills, and finally burned in a local tofu furnace. By setting up the photos and graphs this way, I wanted to make sure that even if a reader is just skimming the page, they can still walk away with a clear understanding of the whole story purely through visuals.

# Final thoughts
I really view this finished site as just Phase 1 of a larger project. If I had the opportunity to keep working on this, I think integrating audio design and soundscapes would be insanely impactful, especially given how tightly this story connects to a musical. There just was not enough time in the day to figure that out right now, and audio work is completely outside my skill set, but it is something I would love to explore down the line. If Basura makes its way to Broadway in the future, I would definitely revisit this site and expand it. I would love to zoom in on high-density regions like India to show the massive volume of waste in populated areas, which was a great suggestion I received in my feedback. Clean, specific data for that is tough to come by and requires more digging (I am surprised I have what I do for this project), but expanding the geographical questions would make the site even better.

Overall, I really enjoyed this course and found this entire project so interesting to build. Learning how to build a real website layout in Shorthand and design charts that actually communicate a story gave me a whole new set of tools. These skills are going to be super helpful for my upcoming capstone project. Seeing how raw data can be turned into an engaging story was awesome, and I am excited to take what I learned here and use it in my future work.
