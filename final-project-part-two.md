# User research 

## Target audience
This project will be written for theater enthusiasts, musicians, environmental advocates, and it should honestly make a scratch at everyday consumers. The story aims to dismantle the myth of what happens to recycling by walking readers through this pipeline.

## Goal 1: Personal Impact & Creative Framing

Ms. I: "Music transforms trash into hope. Seeing instruments built from waste showed me how my daily choices connect to global pollution."

Mr. A: "I skimmed it quickly. Main takeaway I got: the US sends plastic scrap overseas, and some people eat it and some people turn it into music. Cool concept."

## Goal 2: Chart Readability & Visual Layout

# Identified changes for Part III
### Visual & Theme 
* **Add Color:** Shift the Shorthand site colors to be dark in Acts I–III to a bright, vibrant layout in Act IV (*Basura: The Musical*) to mirror the hope.
* **Fix Layout:** Re-format section blocks and visual elements for desktop. try to get to mobile view if there is time.

### Data Visualization & Chart Mechanics
* **Fix Chart Ratios:** Look at the cheat sheet to see how to fix the links that are embeded to look better on Shorthand.
* **Fix the first chart:** Something about the first chart doesn't match the story. We haven't gotten tot hat point yet some maybe a timelog of how long it take to decompose or how much recycle actually says in the US.
* **Add citations into Shorthand:** transfer things from Git Hub onto Shorthand.

### Human-Centered Storytelling & Media
* **Humans:** Embed pictures of the *Basura* cast and the recycled instrument makers.
* **Image Copyright:** Provide citation under all photos.

### Narrative & Policy Balance
* **Personal Action:** Rebalance text to connect individual recycling habits and big scale. Think daily, local, community/movement. 
* **Smooth Transitions:** Insert transition sentences to connect the story to the graphs.
